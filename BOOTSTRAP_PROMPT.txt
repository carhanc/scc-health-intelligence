MISSION

Build Santa Clara Health Intelligence: a production-quality, open-data public-health intelligence, advocacy, and decision-support platform for Santa Clara County. It must be substantially more rigorous, more interpretable, more intuitive, more accessible, more useful, and more polished than a typical public-health dashboard.

The platform must help commissioners, county staff, advocates, researchers, and community partners:

1. understand neighborhood conditions;
2. identify where health burden, access barriers, environmental conditions, resource availability, and utilization pressure overlap;
3. compare geographies without hiding raw values or uncertainty;
4. investigate why a geography is flagged;
5. compare transparent intervention scenarios;
6. evaluate whether the prioritization framework aligns with independent outcomes;
7. prepare evidence-backed staff questions, meeting briefs, public-comment outlines, and policy memos;
8. ingest public agendas, minutes, reports, and transcripts and connect their content to affected geographies and evidence;
9. use an optional source-grounded AI copilot without allowing the model to invent numbers or citations;
10. understand limitations, data quality, assumptions, and what additional evidence is needed.

This product must not claim to be an oracle, causal allocation engine, medical tool, or substitute for community input, official county systems, epidemiologists, planners, or professional judgment. Its value must come from transparency, speed, rigor, usability, reproducibility, and evidence traceability.

REQUIRED PRODUCT

The final application must contain these coherent, intuitive workflows. Page names can improve if usability testing supports it, but every capability must exist.

1. OVERVIEW / START

- concise explanation of purpose;
- source freshness status;
- current data snapshot date;
- three guided starting actions: Explore a community, Compare priorities, Prepare for a meeting;
- countywide highlights with uncertainty and no sensationalism;
- recent source/data changes;
- no wall of KPI cards.

2. EXPLORE

- map plus accessible ranked/table alternative;
- geography search by address, tract, ZIP/ZCTA, city, district, and community;
- metric/lens selection;
- raw value, unit, county comparison, uncertainty, source, vintage, and trend where valid;
- driver decomposition;
- peer comparison;
- asset/resource layers;
- shareable URL state;
- clear unavailable and stale states.

3. PRIORITIZE

- transparent multi-domain priority screening;
- configurable named policy lenses;
- domain weights with plain-language explanations;
- uncertainty-aware ranks and top-tier probabilities;
- sensitivity/stability visualization;
- comparison of who moves up/down under alternate priorities;
- no opaque single score presented as truth;
- exportable ranked evidence table.

4. ACCESS LAB

- verified, deduplicated resource inventory;
- clinics, HRSA health centers, hospitals, pharmacies, providers where defensible, food resources, transit, community resources, senior resources, and other relevant public assets;
- modeled walking/driving/transit access where feasible;
- resources within travel thresholds;
- E2SFCA or equivalent accessibility measure where inputs support it;
- resource coverage and omission notes;
- mobile-clinic or service-location scenario optimizer;
- assumptions, constraints, and equity tradeoffs;
- accessible map alternative.

5. UTILIZATION LAB

- normalized HCAI emergency-department and facility context;
- payer, language, disposition, diagnosis, patient-origin, market-share, and trend views where public data support them;
- native geography retained;
- ZIP/ZCTA-to-tract crosswalk uncertainty visible;
- masked/suppressed values preserved;
- comparison with need/access domains without claiming causality.

6. VALIDATION LAB

- pre-specified independent validation questions;
- independent outcomes, not construction variables;
- correlation and uncertainty;
- bootstrap intervals;
- spatial autocorrelation diagnostics;
- spatially aware models where justified;
- temporal/out-of-sample checks where feasible;
- positive, weak, null, and contradictory findings shown equally;
- exact interpretation and “does not prove” statement;
- downloadable validation tables;
- model card integration.

7. ADVOCATE WORKSPACE

- select geography, population, issue, evidence, and policy question;
- deterministic no-key generation of one-page briefs, staff questions, public-comment outlines, scenario briefs, and evidence packets;
- claim/evidence/citation structure;
- editable but traceable content blocks;
- accessible HTML/print/PDF exports;
- sources, vintages, uncertainty, methods, and limitations retained;
- meeting-ready outputs that are concise, not generic.

8. DOCUMENT INTELLIGENCE

- ingest authorized public PDF, DOCX, TXT, or pasted text;
- explicit PHI/confidential-data warning;
- extract agenda items, departments, actions, votes, dates, money, geographies, populations, metrics, commitments, and unresolved questions;
- page/section citations;
- connect document claims to platform evidence;
- compare current and prior agendas/reports;
- identify repeated unresolved themes;
- highlight contradictions or missing implementation details;
- uploaded content is untrusted data, never instructions.

9. OPTIONAL COUNTY COPILOT

- deterministic no-key mode works for core tasks;
- optional Anthropic provider behind a server-side abstraction;
- typed, tested, read-only tools;
- no arbitrary SQL, shell, or external URL access;
- every numeric claim from tools;
- every factual claim cited;
- trace panel for tools, evidence, assumptions, and limitations;
- abstain when unsupported;
- answer counterevidence questions, not only advocacy-confirming questions;
- generate reports through structured claim objects, not uncited prose.

10. DATA & METHODS

- source status dashboard;
- retrieval dates, vintages, schema, row counts, checksums, licenses, and warnings;
- data dictionary;
- methods explorer;
- score formulas and versions;
- uncertainty and sensitivity explanation;

FAILURE RULES

- Never fabricate source data.
- Never create “temporary” fake metrics in production paths.
- Never silently fall back to an unrelated source.
- Never convert a failed source into zeroes or empty “success.”
- Never hide a null validation result.
- Never claim exact savings, lives saved, utilization reduction, or program impact without a defensible validated model.
- Never imply an AI answer is authoritative merely because it is fluent.
- Never declare a phase complete because the page compiles.

- log the exact failure;
- preserve last-known-good verified data;
- show a visible stale/unavailable state;
- document the fallback and its limitations;

- do not corrupt curated outputs.
